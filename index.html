Council — the Karpathy pipeline: parallel answers, anonymous peer review with scores, then a chairman synthesis. Costs roughly 3–6× a single query, which is the honest tradeoff for the accuracy.
Study — paste a topic and get a worked study pack: orientation, concept map, layered explanation, misconceptions, flashcards you can flip, and a self-check quiz.
Humanize — paste AI-sounding text and get it stripped of the tells: significance inflation, participle padding, rule-of-three, em dash spray, "not just X but Y", boilerplate hedging. It drafts, audits its own draft for what still reads as machine-written, then rewrites. You see all three.
Design notes
  • macOS idiom — vibrancy over flat fills, hairline borders, SF system type, the sliding segmented control, and sheets that spring rather than snap.
  • Jakob's Law — the composer, bubbles and Enter-to-send match every chat app you already use; novelty is spent on the council grid alone.
  • Doherty Threshold — skeletons inside ~100ms, then token streaming, so nothing waits on the slowest seat.
  • Hick's Law — three modes, one composer, one primary action. Configuration hides in a sheet.
  • Von Restorff — the chair's verdict is the only violet, filled surface on the page.
  • Common Region — one card per model with its own colour rail, so attribution never blurs.
  • Fitts's Law — 34–38px targets, edge-pinned, and full-width bottom sheets on phones.
  • Postel's Law — the key field swallows quotes, whitespace and a pasted Bearer prefix.
  • Reduced motion — every animation collapses when the OS asks for it.
